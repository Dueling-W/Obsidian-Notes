## Notes

pg. 17

The effects of small improvements compound over a single year. This shows how even seemingly minor improvements have a huge effect.

pg. 18

"You get what you repeat."

The small actions in our daily lives influence who we will eventually become. The size of the action just determines how quickly we will get there.

pg. 22

Being discouraged by low results just means you are in the plateau, waiting to break out.

pg. 24

"A problem arises when you spend too much time thinking about your goals and not about your systems."

Goals can provide direction but aren't the end all be all. A proper system in-place will naturally lead to success. "Fix the inputs and the outputs will fix themselves."
 
pg. 33

"The more pride you have in a particular aspect of your identity, the more motivated you will be to maintain the habits associated with it."

pg. 39

"You don't need a unanimous vote to win an election; you just need a majority."

This minimizes impossible perfection to our goals. We are not robots. But, if we cast the "right vote" the majority of the time, that is who we will become. For instance, say I study Japanese 4 out of the 7 days, and miss my goal of 5 days. That is still 4/5 days I succeeded, an easy majority.
